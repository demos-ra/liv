Compliance regimes (Phase 4b). Runtime failure behavior across AS v2.2 / Plan v2.2 / M-I v2.3 is governed by two orthogonal regimes declared here for single-source reference. (1) The **error-class regime** partitions failures by recoverability at the point of observation: `CLASS_2` covers output-quality, validation, and capacity-breach errors — resolvable by retry-with-context within a declared budget (M-I IA7a pins capacity-breach to CLASS_2 by axiom); `CLASS_3` covers infrastructure errors — adapter failures, network faults, irrecoverable I/O — resolvable by infra-retry or escalation out of the runtime. The resolution enum (`retried | escalated | terminal`) is captured per-error on `InstanceError.resolution` (M-I §3.5) and feeds IA7b's budget-consumption cardinality. (2) The **breach regime** governs set-level failsafe behavior: any breach of `PerSetLimit` (maxTime, maxTransitions, maxReentries, or a declared `domainFailsafes` rule) short-circuits further dispatch (IA4a) and forces the set to `failed | escalated` (IA4b), overriding any per-declaration retry budget still available. Emission behavior under both regimes is unchanged — M-I still produces a `RunReportContent` via AS v2.2 carrying the observed outcome, errors, and violations; AS's own gates (ArA1–ArA8) do not distinguish success from failure outcomes, only whether the emission itself is structurally valid. Consumers reading a RunReport decide retry / replan / abandon at their own layer; the runtime's job is to emit truthfully.
