### §3.2 Vision-tier prose vs property-naming addendum — boundary question

There is a real question: does the phantom-layer addendum's existence in Part I (a "vision-tier" part per the Manual's own structure — Parts I–IV introduce vision-shaped declarations; Operations is the prose-deferral zone) cross the line from "property-naming" to "vision amendment"?

2. **The addendum names what was implicit at v13.3, not a new claim.** The substrate at v13.3 already accepted any typed signal honestly (substrate "stores what arrives honestly" per existing L96 prose). The apparatus at v13.3 already emitted system-operation records via internal-derivation channel (per Path B framing at L174). The addendum is mechanically a property-naming amendment that happens to live in Part I because Part I is where substrate-tier structural properties belong per the Manual's own discipline.

### §3.3 Phantom-layer terminology — minimum-novelty discipline

Considered whether "phantom layer" is the right term, given it is a new term not previously in the Manual. Three candidates considered:

- **(a) "the phantom layer"** — spec W2's explicit phrasing.
- **(b) "the system-introspection layer"** — descriptive, no novelty.
- **(c) no named property; just declare the three structural claims without a label.**

Selected **(a)** for two reasons:
1. **Spec W2 prescribes the phrase.** "Phantom layer named: system-introspection content is the phantom layer." The spec is explicit.
2. **The term names the load-bearing property: structurally-present-but-anchor-excluded.** A "layer" that is "phantom" is present but does not register at the layer above (anchor-derivation). This captures the precise mechanic. Calling it "the system-introspection layer" obscures the load-bearing property (phantom = present-but-anchor-excluded); calling it nothing leaves the property un-named, which is exactly what spec W2 mandates against.

**Carry-cost.** "Phantom layer" is a new term added to the Manual at Shot 3. It does NOT enter the canonical-naming table (it's a structural property of Part I, not a canonical-name vocabulary item). It is declared at the subsection heading + addendum body and lives there as a Part-I-internal term. Phase D and beyond consume "phantom layer" by reference if they want to use it; otherwise, the canonical-naming row's ContentClass terminology suffices.
